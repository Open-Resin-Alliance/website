The Open Resin Alliance was founded on 15 August 2024. It is a group of hobbyists and developers from around the globe, joined in the shared goal of advancing the field of resin-based 3D printing through open-source collaboration. Everything the organisation publishes lives on GitHub at [github.com/Open-Resin-Alliance](https://github.com/Open-Resin-Alliance), and the work is done in the open from the first commit.

## What the alliance works on

In the organisation's own words, its projects are "primarily software and reference designs for open-source resin 3d printing". That covers everything between a resin printer and a finished print: the software that prepares models and supports, the software that drives the machine, and the file-format work that lets the two talk to each other.

The belief behind the alliance is equally direct. By sharing knowledge and resources, the community can accelerate innovation and make high-quality 3D printing accessible to everyone. Licences are chosen per project, the repositories are public, and the roadmap is discussed in issues and pull requests rather than behind closed doors.

## Two projects that predate the org

Two of the projects now hosted here are older than the organisation itself. Odyssey, the engine for processing Prusa SL1 slicer files for the Apollo series of control boards, was created on 3 January 2023. Orion, the frontend designed to control it, followed on 13 January 2023 and is tailored to run on a wide variety of devices, primarily Linux single-board computers.

Both began life under TheContrappostoShop. The organisation's profile notes that "Some of these projects have grown and moved from TheContrappostoShop to the ORA, to better serve the community at large." That move reflects what the alliance is for: giving long-running open-source projects a stable, collaborative home, and giving users a single place to follow them.

## What open source means here

Open source is not only about publishing a licence file. Discussions happen in public issues, changes arrive as pull requests, and releases are tagged and documented on the repositories. That makes the work auditable and makes it possible for anyone to pick up a bug, a translation, or a platform they care about.

## Get involved

The fastest way in is the community Discord at [discord.gg/beFeTaPH6v](https://discord.gg/beFeTaPH6v), where the people working on these projects answer questions and talk through ideas. If you would rather start from the code, browse the repositories at [github.com/Open-Resin-Alliance](https://github.com/Open-Resin-Alliance) and open an issue or a pull request on whichever project interests you. Contributions of every size are useful, whether they are fixes, documentation, or a report from a printer nobody has tried yet.
