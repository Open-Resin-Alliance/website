DragonFruit v0.1.16 was published on 6 October 2026 as a dev build. The tag is at [github.com/Open-Resin-Alliance/DragonFruit/releases/tag/v0.1.16](https://github.com/Open-Resin-Alliance/DragonFruit/releases/tag/v0.1.16).

## What a dev build is

DragonFruit ships development snapshots rather than stable releases, and v0.1.16 is one. Its release notes call it "a big one" and repeat the standing disclaimer, which is worth reading before installing rather than clicking past:

> v0.1.16 is a dev build, i.e. a snapshot of the `dev` branch, and it's the bleeding edge of DragonFruit development. We regularly release them to show feature previews, bug fixes, and to stabilize the dev tree towards the next stable release. We firmly believe in development in the open.
>
> It's expected that development releases can be buggy, unstable and may cause data loss, crashes and potentially hardware damage. Dev builds are only targeted for advanced users wanting to be at the very tip of the edge, and/or wanting to help with DragonFruit development.
>
> Regular users are expected to move only from stable to stable release. Install it under your responsibility. We make no promises. Got it?

The version number encodes the channel. DragonFruit uses an odd/even minor convention: `dev` carries the odd line (`0.1.x`, `0.3.x`) and `main` the stable one (`0.2.x`, `0.4.x`), and a dev-line build is neither eligible to be the repository's "Latest release" nor wired into the stable updater feed. v0.1.16 is marked as a prerelease, so it is a snapshot on the way to the first stable line rather than a build to hand to someone who wants predictability.

## Two generations of auto-supports

The largest change is the second time the automatic support generator has been reworked from what printing it taught the team. The notes describe both generations together:

> **Auto-Supports v1.6 and v2.0**: two rounds of rework on automatic supports. Contacts are now sized by height and area, long spans become real branches instead of tapered cones, and there's a new zigzag bracing pattern. v2.0 judges pose stability (topple margin, adhesion) before adding anti-topple coverage, uses braces instead of support carpets on large flats, and runs on a worker thread with progress and coverage shown in the modal.

The first generation is about the supports themselves: contacts sized from what they carry, spans that branch where they are long instead of tapering into a cone, and a zigzag bracing pattern between them. The second is about the part as a whole - whether the pose can topple or slide off the plate, judged before anything is added to prevent it, with braces rather than full carpets over large flat areas. Because it is a heavier pass, v2.0 runs on a worker thread and reports progress and coverage from the modal. The same release fixes grid parenting, shallow branch angles near existing supports, and minima lost under voxel islands.

## Auto-orientation and support blockers

Orientation is no longer a manual search. Auto-Orientation v1.0 offers deterministic orientation with selectable objectives and a one-click apply, which is a different kind of tool from the supports themselves - it decides how the model sits, and then supports follow. Support blockers round it out: no-go areas painted on a model that automatic supports refuse and that auto-orientation steers away from the plate.

## One 45-degree diagonal instead of a routing engine

Support routing was replaced rather than tuned. Trunks now take a single 45-degree diagonal and then drop straight to the plate, which retires the multi-joint routing engines and their Rust crates - code the notes describe as having "failed to reach some contacts and produced odd shapes". Contact cones are collision-checked again, a check the removed machinery had been carrying poorly.

## Parts resting on the plate

A part that sits flat on the plate is its own awkward case, because there is almost no overhang to detect and no room underneath to reach. This release works on it from three sides: the raft is trimmed around models, automatic supports cover domed undersides that touch the plate instead of skipping them, and stumps no longer poke into the model.

## Baked ambient occlusion and a camera that holds its framing

Ambient occlusion is now computed natively per vertex, so crevices and fine relief read properly instead of washing out, and it is controlled from the Mesh tab. The release adds a view mode menu, two built-in themes, and selection and hover tints as theme colors alongside it. The camera work is the other half of the viewport story: a real orthographic dolly that keeps your framing when you switch projection, SpaceMouse fixes under ortho, quarter-turn arrows and a home button on the viewcube, and smoother trackpad pan and orbit.

## Slicing

- **Faster dithering.** Floyd-Steinberg now only walks the part of each row that can change, which makes slices 2x faster or more for models that do not fill the plate.
- **3DAA support tip shrink.** Support contacts are shrunk at slice time, 10% by default and configurable, to compensate for the stronger cure. The viewport keeps showing the authored size, so the scene and the slice differ deliberately.
- **A new output format.** The LumenFormat plugin adds a `.lumen` output, which can embed the VOXL scene in the print file.
- **Materials.** The Siraya Tech library ships 1,790 CBX 50µm presets across 50 Anycubic, Elegoo and UniFormation printers, alongside a redesigned Material Library.
- **CLI parity.** `scene slice` now builds exactly the same slice job as the application, anti-aliasing included.
- **Everyday handling.** Autosave takes a configurable cooldown, three minutes by default, instead of writing repeatedly while you navigate, and VOXL saves keep which geometry is model and which is supports. Loaded plates are snappier - duplicating, hovering and selecting no longer pay for the whole support set, and draw calls are batched.

## Around the application

The updater was refreshed: you can switch to the dev channel from inside the application, the changelog it shows is the real one rather than a placeholder, and update notifications behave differently. Sponsors get tiered rings in Settings → About, which follows the sponsors carousel the previous build added. The RTSP camera relay now detects when a printer runs out of stream slots and reclaims stale sessions instead of wedging it. Translation work landed across dialogs, Support Studio, auto-supports, islands, organic cut and the updater - the message catalogue grew from 693 entries at v0.1.15 to 1,279 at v0.1.16, with Czech, German, Spanish, French and Slovak catalogues alongside English - and CI now rejects new UI strings that arrive untranslated.

## Fixes

Two of these affect whether the application starts at all. On Windows, importing meshes could crash because of an outdated Microsoft C++ runtime; the installer now requires a compatible one and offers to install it. On x86-64, the build dropped its AVX2 requirement, so DragonFruit runs again on CPUs from before 2013 - benchmarks showed the flag was giving no measurable speedup, because it had never actually made it into the generated assembly.

The slicer fixes are a list of crashes and edge cases: slices could crash from parallel blurs overwriting each other's buffer, slicing hollowed models could crash from a corrupted mesh buffer, sub-pixel features could streak long scanlines, models fully outside the build volume are now excluded, and models crossing the edge are clipped correctly. Dithering was downgrading 8-bit panels to 7-bit, and the PNG compression setting was ignored. Support editing got four repairs: branches can attach to a selected curved segment, joints can be added below branches and leaves, branch tips no longer snap back after dragging, and stumps respect the simplified render and root settings. LYS import rebuilds mini supports, sticks, braces and kickstands correctly and guards against corrupted files. Removing a hollow now fully restores the previous state with undo and redo staying consistent. On Linux, closing the application no longer asks "Leave this site?". Ctrl+A no longer opens Arrange, selection and hover colors survive a reload, and several orthographic picking and island-display defects are gone.

## Under the hood

The changes behind the interface matter for what comes next. The support system is now driven by a single type registry, removing about 2,000 hand-written per-type references. A dead-code sweep took out a second, unused island scan stack of about 3,900 lines, a broken 3MF export path that was never called, and unused npm and Rust dependencies including a 44 MB `ffmpeg-static`. Slice job assembly moved into one module shared by the application and the CLI, covered by golden tests, and the CLI is now 1.0.0. There is a new slicing benchmark suite, and CTB output became reproducible through `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH`.

The build and CI story is less visible but just as real: macOS universal builds link in parallel, the Tauri CEF line moved to alpha.26, Next.js is at 16.3.8, CI caching got faster, the preview pipeline was hardened, issues are labelled automatically, and the documentation was consolidated under `docs/`. One fix is worth calling out because it is the end of a story this blog covered in v0.1.15: the release script no longer republishes over an already-published release.
